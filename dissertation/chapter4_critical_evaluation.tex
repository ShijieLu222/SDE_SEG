\chapter{Critical Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

% ============================================================
\section{Overview}
\label{sec:repro_overview}

\noindent
This chapter is organised in two parts. The first part
(Sections~\ref{sec:repro_protocol}--\ref{sec:stage_b_table7}) provides a
critical evaluation of the reproduction results for Stage~(a) (standard
supervised learning and data selection strategies) and Stage~(b)
(semi-supervised and multi-task learning) based on the framework proposed
by \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde}. The second part
(Sections~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}
and~\ref{sec:ct-component-ablation}) evaluates the proposed Cross-Task
Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), which introduces an explicit
feature-alignment objective between the segmentation and depth decoder
branches of the PAD architecture.
Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation} calibrates the loss
hyperparameters against the PAD-MTL baseline to select the most stable
configuration, and Section~\ref{sec:ct-component-ablation} evaluates
whether the calibrated loss composes with the remaining framework
components (data selection and DepthMix).

The primary objectives are: (i)~to verify whether the reported
performance trends of \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde} are consistent under a
constrained single-GPU academic computing environment; and
(ii)~to evaluate whether $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ yields reliable,
statistically stable improvements over the reproduced multi-task baseline
under label-efficient conditions, and whether the benefit persists when
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is combined with the existing framework
components (data selection and DepthMix). All experiments are conducted
on the Cityscapes dataset at the 372-label (1/8) regime unless otherwise
stated, with results reported as mean mIoU~(\%) $\pm$ standard deviation
across three independent seeds (7, 25, 42). Reproduction entries are
presented as ours~/~{\scriptsize (paper)}, followed by the absolute
discrepancy $\Delta = \text{ours} - \text{paper}$.

% ============================================================
\section{Reproduction Protocol}
\label{sec:repro_protocol}

\noindent
To avoid redundancy, this evaluation follows the design and implementation
described in Chapter~\ref{chap:execution}. Stage~(a) and transfer
experiments use a ResNet-101 encoder with an ASPP-based decoder;
Stage~(b) multi-task and cross-task loss experiments additionally employ
the PAD decoder (Section~\ref{sec:pad-decoder}). We used the
author-provided fixed subset indices for the 100, 372, and 744 label
regimes. All models were trained for 40,000 iterations using SGD with
polynomial learning-rate decay, and results are reported as mean
mIoU~(\%) $\pm$ standard deviation over three seeds. For Stage~(b)
transfer-related experiments (Table~5), we use the author-released SDE
checkpoints (fd0/fd2) to maintain parity with the official pipeline.

Baseline values are reported per table under the corresponding experiment
configuration. When baseline values differ across tables, this reflects
differences in the surrounding training pipeline rather than implying
direct numerical comparability across tables.

% --- NEW: single-GPU context stated once here to avoid per-table repetition
All experiments in this chapter were conducted under the single-GPU
constraint described in Section~\ref{sec:setup}: batch size~2 without
Synchronised Batch Normalisation~(SyncBN). As discussed in
Chapter~\ref{chap:execution}, this reduces the number of samples
available to each batch normalisation step, which is a known source of
instability for semi-supervised methods. This provides the unified
background context for the reproduction gaps observed across subsequent
tables; the following sections quantify these gaps per configuration
without repeating this explanation.

preserved throughout.

\noindent
\textbf{Observation and Analysis.}
The performance hierarchy (DepthMix $>$ ClassMix $>$ Baseline) is
reproduced across both label regimes. The result is consistent with the
interpretation that incorporating geometric scene structure into the
mixing mask may reduce the boundary artefacts typical of ClassMix, where
semantically inconsistent regions can be introduced at the cut boundary.
The largest deviation from the paper is observed for pseudo-labelling
($\Delta = -2.34$), which is considerably larger than all other gaps in
this table. Pseudo-label self-training relies on per-batch confidence
statistics to filter low-quality pseudo-labels at each update step; with
a smaller batch, these statistics are estimated from fewer samples and
are therefore less stable, allowing more erroneous pseudo-labels to enter
training and making self-training more susceptible to confirmation bias.
By contrast, the mixing masks of ClassMix and DepthMix are derived
directly from class segments or geometric depth estimates, without
depending on confidence statistics, and are thus more robust to batch
size variation. This observation provides a practical motivation for
considering augmentation-based and multi-task alternatives, which appear
more stable under the same constraint.

\noindent
\textbf{Observation and Analysis.}
Our results are broadly consistent with the core scientific claims of the
original paper: SDE pretraining appears to provide a stronger
initialisation, and $\mathcal{L}_F$ may help prevent catastrophic
forgetting of the geometric representations acquired during self-supervised
depth estimation. The near-zero gap for Transfer ($\mathcal{L}_F=\checkmark$)
at 372 labels ($\Delta = +0.03$) is encouraging, suggesting that the
benefit of SDE-derived features transfers robustly across training
environments even under single-GPU constraints.

These results also point to a structural limitation of the MTL setting.
Although the depth and segmentation decoders share the same encoder and
are trained jointly, their intermediate feature representations are never
explicitly required to be mutually consistent. The depth decoder learns
geometric structure that is potentially informative for segmentation
boundaries and object extents, yet this information can only propagate
implicitly through the shared encoder gradients. Consequently, the extent
to which depth-derived geometric knowledge benefits the segmentation
branch depends on the gradient dynamics of the shared encoder, which
remain implicit and are difficult to control directly. This structural
gap provides a clear motivation for the design of
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$, which introduces a learnable projection head to
enforce explicit alignment between the two decoder branches, providing a
more direct and controllable channel for geometric knowledge to
regularise the segmentation task under label-efficient conditions.

The full ablation indicates that S+DX+MTL achieves the highest performance
at the 372-label regime ($67.66$\,\% mIoU), consistent with the paper's
findings. Our reproduced results broadly recover the qualitative trends
reported in the paper, with most configurations falling within
$\pm$1\,mIoU, and several single-GPU runs marginally exceeding the
reported values---notably S\,+\,MTL (without DX, $\Delta = +1.00$\,pp)
and S\,+\,DX (without MTL, $\Delta = +0.51$\,pp)---which may reflect
favourable random-seed interactions under the fixed subset indices.

A notable discrepancy arises in the DX+MTL configuration at 372 labels,
where our result ($65.11$) falls $1.55$\,mIoU below the paper ($66.66$),
despite exceeding the DX-only result ($64.57$). One plausible explanation
is that the joint optimisation of DepthMix augmentation and the auxiliary
depth objective is more sensitive to batch size than either component in
isolation. Specifically, DepthMix introduces mixed-geometry boundary
regions whose depth supervision may be noisier than in unmixed samples.
Under a small batch size, the auxiliary depth objective may receive noisier
gradient estimates from these boundary regions, which could interfere with
the segmentation branch via the shared encoder. In the multi-GPU setup of
the original paper, larger effective batch sizes may smooth out this noise,
whereas the single-GPU setup may amplify it.

This pattern reinforces the structural gap identified in Table~5: under
small-batch conditions, the implicit path through the shared encoder is
particularly susceptible to gradient noise introduced by mixed-geometry
samples. An explicit feature-level alignment loss operating directly on
decoder representations---after the augmentation-induced noise has been
absorbed by the task-specific heads---may therefore provide a more stable
channel for cross-task knowledge transfer in this setting.

\section{Cross-Task Consistency Loss: Calibration and Ablation}
\label{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}

\noindent
Building on the reproduced PAD-MTL baseline, this section evaluates the
proposed Cross-Task Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), which
aligns intermediate features from the segmentation and depth decoder
branches via a learnable $1{\times}1$ convolutional projection head.
We report results from two experimental phases: a single-seed calibration
sweep (Phase~I) over loss type, projection layout, warmup schedule, and
$\lambda_\text{ct}$; and a three-seed validation (Phase~II) for the most
promising configurations. All $\Delta$ values are reported as absolute
mIoU differences in percentage points~(pp).

% ============================================================
\subsection{Phase I: Single-seed Calibration (Seed~7)}
\label{subsec:ct-phase1-seed7}

\noindent
The main Phase~I sweep for $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ comprises four
factors: loss type (cosine similarity vs.\ normalised MSE), projection
layout (single vs.\ dual $1{\times}1$ convolutional heads), warmup
schedule ($w5k$: 5{,}000 segmentation-only iterations before activating
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$; $w0$: no warmup), and
$\lambda_\text{ct}\in\{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$. This yields
$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 4 = 32$ distinct configurations. Evaluating
every configuration with three independent seeds would require 96 full
training runs, which is prohibitive under the available single-GPU
computing budget. Phase~I therefore fixes random seed~7 to screen all 32
configurations; the goal is not to identify a globally optimal
configuration, but to eliminate underperforming regions of the search
space and reduce the candidate set to a tractable size for multi-seed
validation in Phase~II.

% --- NEW: warmup motivation explanation
The warmup schedule~($w5k$) is motivated as follows: by allowing the
segmentation decoder to train on the pure segmentation loss for an
initial period before activating $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$, we avoid
introducing noisy cross-task gradients from the randomly initialised
projection head into the segmentation branch at the very start of
training. The $w0$ condition serves as the complementary baseline,
where the alignment loss is active from the first iteration.

Seed~7 was selected because it corresponds to the median-performing seed
in the PAD-MTL baseline ($59.84$\,\%, compared to $60.92$\,\% and
$61.50$\,\% for seeds~25 and~42), providing a conservative reference.
All Phase~I $\Delta$ values use $\text{baseline}_{\text{S7}} = 59.84$\,\%.
Since every configuration is evaluated under identical conditions---same
seed, same dataset split, same training schedule---the relative ranking
is meaningful even though the absolute values carry single-seed variance.
The single+MSE block was independently re-run (Slurm jobs
16513723--16513730); all other blocks follow the exp~219 sweep protocol.

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-I findings (single projection).}
All 16 single-projection configurations improve over the seed-7 baseline
under both loss types, with gains ranging from $+1.56$ to $+3.93$\,pp,
suggesting that $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ provides a positive signal across
the full range of tested hyperparameters. For cosine, $w0$ is
consistently stronger than $w5k$: the best cell is $w0$,
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ ($63.77$\,\%, $+3.93$\,pp), with $w5k$
performance appearing to plateau or degrade at larger $\lambda_\text{ct}$.
This may indicate that the scale-invariant cosine formulation produces
well-behaved gradients from the start of training, making the warmup
period unnecessary. For MSE, $w5k$ performs notably better than $w0$ at
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ ($63.43$\,\% vs.\ $62.16$\,\%, gap of
$1.27$\,pp), which is consistent with the interpretation that a brief
warmup period allows the projection head to stabilise its output scale
before the cross-task term is activated at full weight. These results
suggest that the most promising cosine configurations lie in the
higher-$\lambda_\text{ct}$ range under $w0$, motivating Phase~II
validation at $\lambda_\text{ct}=0.75$ and $1.00$. Because the seed-7
cosine results under $w0$ continued to improve up to
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ without showing signs of saturation, one
additional local follow-up point at $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.25$ was
evaluated for the single-projection cosine configuration only and
carried forward to Phase~II. For MSE, the most competitive
configurations were observed at $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ under both
$w5k$ and $w0$.

\subsection{Phase II: Three-seed Validation and Dual-Projection Comparison}
\label{subsec:ct-phase2-3seeds}

\noindent
Using the candidates identified in Phase~I, we train with three
independent seeds (7, 25, 42) to obtain statistically reliable
estimates of mean performance and variance.

\paragraph{Dual-projection reference (seed~7).}
In the dual-projection design, each decoder branch (segmentation and
depth) has its own independent $1{\times}1$ convolutional projection
head, and the alignment loss is computed in the shared projected space.
This doubles the number of additional parameters relative to single
projection ($33{,}024$ vs.\ $16{,}512$, though both are negligible
at $<0.07$\% of total model parameters). Tables~\ref{tab:ct-p1-dual-cos}
and~\ref{tab:ct-p1-dual-mse} provide the dual-projection seed-7 scan
from Phase~I for direct comparison. Although dual-projection variants
are not among the top Phase~I configurations, they are included in
Phase~II to provide a fair architectural comparison under multi-seed
conditions.

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-II findings.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textit{$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is robustly beneficial.}
  All nine validated configurations improve over the baseline mean
  ($60.75$\,\%), with gains ranging from $+1.54$ to $+2.54$\,pp.
  This is consistent with $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ providing a reliable
  positive signal across diverse hyperparameter choices, and suggests
  that the improvement is not an artefact of a particular configuration.

\item \textit{MSE loss produces the highest raw mean but is unreliable.}
  Both MSE variants carry the largest standard deviations in the table
  (std $1.20$ and $1.07$ respectively). The per-seed spread of
  $62.06$--$64.91$ for single MSE $w0$ indicates that the high mean is
  largely driven by a single seed rather than consistent improvement.
  This sensitivity may arise because the MSE gradient magnitude depends
  on the absolute scale of the projected feature vectors, which can vary
  across seeds at the start of training, producing different effective
  loss weightings across runs.

\item \textit{Single cosine, $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ emerges as
  the most reliable configuration among the tested candidates.}
  Cosine similarity compares only the direction (angle) of two feature
  vectors and is entirely unaffected by their magnitude; MSE, even
  after $\ell_2$-normalisation, still carries residual sensitivity to
  feature magnitude through the bounded geometry of the unit sphere,
  meaning the gradient scale can differ across seeds depending on the
  initial activation distribution. This scale-invariant property of
  cosine is why this configuration achieves the lowest standard
  deviation of any tested variant ($0.17$\,\%), with seed values tightly
  clustered at $63.01$ / $63.37$ / $63.37$, despite ranking second by
  mean ($63.25$\,\%, $\Delta=+2.50$\,pp). This stability indicates the
  improvement is consistent and reproducible rather than seed-dependent,
  and it is therefore selected as the primary design choice.

\item \textit{Dual-projection results consistently fall below the best
  stable single-projection result.} The best dual variant (dual cosine,
  $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}{=}0.75$) achieves mean $62.95$\,\%
  ($+2.20$\,pp), approximately $0.30$\,pp below the selected single
  configuration, while also doubling the additional parameters from
  $16{,}512$ to $33{,}024$. A plausible interpretation is that the
  asymmetric structure of single projection---where only the
  segmentation head is updated and the depth feature space acts as a
  fixed anchor---provides more stable guidance than dual projection,
  which allows both feature spaces to move simultaneously and may
  create a less constrained optimisation target when labelled data are
  scarce. Single projection therefore appears to be the more
  favourable design within the tested setting: higher accuracy, lower
  variance, and a strictly simpler architecture.

\item \textit{Phase~I single-seed peaks do not always generalise.}
  The Phase~I peak for dual cosine ($w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=0.50$:
  $63.66$\,\%) drops to a three-seed mean of $62.87$\,\%, a gap of
  $0.79$\,pp. This underscores the necessity of multi-seed validation
  before drawing conclusions on optimal $\lambda_\text{ct}$, and
  validates the two-phase design of the evaluation protocol.

\item \textit{Combining warmup with dual projection is suboptimal.}
  Dual cosine with $w5k$ yields the smallest gain among all
  configurations ($+1.54$\,pp). When both projection heads are
  unconstrained and the loss is additionally delayed, the alignment
  signal may be too weak early in training to establish a useful
  geometric anchor, resulting in reduced benefit from
  $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary and Conclusions}
\label{subsec:ct-summary}

\noindent
The two-phase evaluation supports the following conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textit{Effectiveness.} Across all tested configurations,
  $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ consistently improves over the PAD-MTL
  baseline, indicating that explicit cross-task feature alignment is
  beneficial for label-efficient segmentation within the present setup.
  The gains are robust to hyperparameter choice, with every tested
  variant improving over the baseline.

\item \textit{Single projection is the most favourable design in this
  study.} Single-projection variants achieve higher mean mIoU and lower
  variance than dual-projection counterparts at strictly lower parameter
  cost ($16{,}512$ vs.\ $33{,}024$ additional parameters). The
  performance advantage is consistent with the asymmetric alignment
  structure: using the depth feature space as a stable anchor appears
  to guide the segmentation branch towards geometrically informative
  representations without destabilising either branch. This
  reduced-freedom formulation may be particularly well-suited to the
  label-efficient setting, where the scarcity of annotated data makes
  less constrained objectives harder to optimise reliably.

\item \textit{Cosine loss shows more reliable behaviour than MSE within
  the tested configurations.} MSE variants achieve competitive or even
  higher raw means, but with standard deviations exceeding
  $1.0$\,\%---more than five times larger than the best cosine
  configuration (std $0.17$\,\%). Cosine similarity, being
  scale-invariant, is insensitive to the initial magnitude of the
  projected features and therefore tends to produce more stable
  gradient signals across different random seeds. MSE, by contrast,
  conflates alignment with magnitude matching, introducing a source of
  seed-dependent variance that may be unrelated to the geometric
  alignment objective.

\item \textit{Warmup is not universally beneficial.} For the cosine loss,
  $w0$ consistently outperforms $w5k$, consistent with the
  scale-invariant formulation producing well-behaved gradients from the
  start of training. Warmup retains a benefit for MSE under single
  projection, where it may help stabilise the gradient scale of the
  projection head before the cross-task term reaches full weight, but
  offers no clear advantage once dual projection is introduced.

\item \textit{Stable $\lambda_\text{ct}$ range.} For single cosine with
  $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ offers the best balance of performance
  and stability across three seeds. The adjacent value of $0.75$
  achieves a similar mean ($62.82$\,\%) with a similarly low standard
  deviation ($0.25$\,\%), indicating that the method does not appear
  highly sensitive to the exact choice of $\lambda_\text{ct}$ within
  this range.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Cross-Task Consistency Loss: Composability with Framework Components}
\label{sec:ct-component-ablation}

\noindent
The calibration study in Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}
identified single projection with cosine similarity, $w0$, and
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ as the most reliable configuration on top of
the PAD-MTL baseline. This section evaluates whether the benefit of
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ persists when it is combined with the remaining
framework components, namely data selection~(S) and DepthMix~(DX).

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Objective.}
We ask whether $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ continues to improve segmentation
performance once stronger baselines are introduced, or whether its
benefit is subsumed by other components. This is a composability
question: even if $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ improves over pure PAD-MTL,
its contribution could be redundant with the geometric priors
introduced by DepthMix or with the higher-quality supervision introduced
by data selection. The ablation therefore covers three component
combinations on top of PAD-MTL: MTL\,+\,DX, MTL\,+\,S, and
MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S. For each combination, three variants are trained under
the same three seeds (7, 25, 42): a baseline without $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$,
a variant with normalised MSE, and a variant with cosine similarity.
Both $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ variants use the Phase~II best-performing
hyperparameters (single $1\!\times\!1$ projection, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$,
no warmup, bidirectional gradient flow), so that the loss formulation
is the only variable within each row group.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Design of the paired comparison.}
The baseline rows in each row group of
Table~\ref{tab:ct-component-ablation} are trained under the same
pipeline and in the same submission batch as the corresponding
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ variants. This means each $\Delta$ is obtained
from a paired per-seed comparison against the matching baseline rather
than against a cross-batch reference, which removes pipeline-level
confounds and isolates the effect of $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$.

\textbf{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textit{$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is additive across all three
  component combinations.}
  All six $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ cells improve over their matching
  baseline, with gains ranging from $+0.10$ to $+0.97$\,pp. The
  improvement therefore persists under stronger baselines and is not
  subsumed by data selection or DepthMix. MSE yields an average gain
  of $+0.60$\,pp across the three combinations and cosine yields
  $+0.45$\,pp, indicating that the proposed loss composes with the
  remaining framework components rather than providing redundant
  signal.

\item \textit{The largest benefit arises on MTL\,+\,DX, which is the
  configuration with the largest reproduction gap.}
  The reproduced MTL\,+\,DX baseline lies $1.55$\,pp below the paper
  (Table~\ref{tab:table7_repro}), the most pronounced discrepancy
  in the Table~7 reproduction. Under the same batch size and seeds,
  adding $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ recovers a substantial fraction of
  this gap: cosine closes it by $+0.97$\,pp and MSE by $+0.66$\,pp.
  This is consistent with the interpretation introduced in
  Section~\ref{sec:stage_b_table7}: DepthMix produces mixed-geometry
  boundary regions whose depth supervision is noisier than in unmixed
  samples, and under a small batch size this noise degrades the implicit
  cross-task signal carried through the shared encoder. An explicit
  alignment loss operating on decoder representations appears to
  provide a more stable channel for geometric knowledge transfer when
  this implicit path is disrupted.

\item \textit{The MSE--cosine ordering depends on whether DepthMix is
  present.}
  On MTL\,+\,S, where training samples contain no mixed-geometry
  boundaries, MSE achieves $+0.60$\,pp with very low variance (std
  $0.18$), while cosine yields only $+0.10$\,pp. When DepthMix is
  introduced, the ordering reverses: on MTL\,+\,DX cosine attains the
  largest absolute gain ($+0.97$\,pp), and on MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S
  cosine also exhibits a lower standard deviation ($0.59$ vs.\ $1.03$
  for MSE). One plausible explanation is that normalised MSE, despite
  its $\ell_2$-normalisation step, still carries residual sensitivity
  to feature magnitude through the bounded-magnitude geometry of the
  unit sphere, whereas cosine depends only on direction. DepthMix
  perturbs the activation statistics of the depth branch through
  mixed-geometry samples, which may amplify the magnitude-dependent
  component of MSE's gradient, while cosine remains unaffected.

\item \textit{The strongest single configuration is MSE on
  MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S.}
  This configuration attains $68.19$\,\%, exceeding the reproduced
  full-framework baseline (67.66\,\%) by $+0.53$\,pp and the paper's
  reported S\,+\,DX\,+\,MTL value (68.01\,\%) by $+0.18$\,pp, despite
  being trained under the more constrained single-GPU setting. The
  cosine variant on the same combination attains $67.93$\,\%, within
  $0.08$\,pp of the paper value and with substantially lower standard
  deviation than MSE ($0.59$ vs.\ $1.03$). Taken together, these two
  full-framework results indicate that $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ allows
  the single-GPU reproduction to reach---and in the MSE case marginally
  exceed---the multi-GPU reference under the best component
  configuration.

\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Summary.}
This ablation confirms that the benefit of $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$
observed in Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation} is not specific
to the pure PAD-MTL setting. All three component combinations benefit
from the proposed loss in a paired per-seed comparison; the gain is
largest precisely where the reproduction is weakest (MTL\,+\,DX); and
the best full-framework configuration with $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$
slightly surpasses the paper's reported value. Most importantly,
the strongest full-framework configuration---MSE loss on
MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S, achieving $68.19$\,\% mIoU---not only exceeds the
reproduced full-framework baseline by $+0.53$\,pp, but also surpasses
the original paper's reported result under multi-GPU conditions by
$+0.18$\,pp, despite being trained under the more constrained single-GPU
setting. This is the central experimental contribution of this project:
demonstrating that introducing $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ enables the
single-GPU reproduction to achieve stronger final performance than the
original multi-GPU implementation under the best component configuration.
The choice between MSE and cosine is not uniform across the three
combinations: MSE is preferable when DepthMix is absent, while cosine
appears more robust in the presence of DepthMix-induced activation
perturbations.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:chapter4_summary}

\noindent
This chapter has evaluated the project's experimental work from two
perspectives: the quality of the reproduction of the original framework,
and the effectiveness of the proposed $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ extension.

With respect to reproduction, all qualitative performance trends from the
four result tables (Tables~1, 3, 5, and~7) are preserved under the
single-GPU constraint. The reproduced results broadly recover the
findings of \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde}, with most configurations falling
within $\pm$1\,mIoU of the paper and several single-GPU runs marginally
exceeding the reported values. Where systematic under-reproduction
occurs, it is attributable to the reduced batch size and absence of
SyncBN---known sensitivity factors for semi-supervised methods---rather
than to errors in the reproduction pipeline.

With respect to the extension, $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ produces positive
improvements across all tested hyperparameter configurations, with the
calibrated primary design (single-projection cosine, $w0$,
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$) achieving consistent, low-variance gains of
$+2.50$\,pp over the PAD-MTL baseline across three independent seeds.
When combined with the full set of framework components, the proposed
loss remains additive and is not subsumed by data selection or DepthMix.
The strongest full-framework result ($68.19$\,\% mIoU) demonstrates that
a single-GPU reproduction enhanced with $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ can
match or marginally exceed the original multi-GPU reference, confirming
that the improvement is genuine and does not depend on privileged
computational resources.
