\chapter{Critical Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

% ============================================================
\section{Overview}
\label{sec:repro_overview}

\noindent
This chapter is organised in two parts. The first part
(Sections~\ref{sec:repro_protocol}--\ref{sec:stage_b_table7}) provides a
critical evaluation of the reproduction results for Stage~(a) (standard
supervised learning and data selection strategies) and Stage~(b)
(semi-supervised and multi-task learning) based on the framework proposed
by \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde}. The second part
(Sections~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}
and~\ref{sec:ct-component-ablation}) evaluates the proposed Cross-Task
Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), which introduces an explicit
feature-alignment objective between the segmentation and depth decoder
branches of the PAD architecture.
Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation} calibrates the loss
hyperparameters against the PAD-MTL baseline to select the most stable
configuration, and Section~\ref{sec:ct-component-ablation} evaluates
whether the calibrated loss composes with the remaining framework
components (data selection and DepthMix).

The primary objectives are: (i)~to verify whether the reported
performance trends of \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde} are consistent under a
constrained single-GPU academic computing environment; and
(ii)~to evaluate whether $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ yields reliable,
seed-consistent improvements over the reproduced multi-task baseline
under label-efficient conditions, and whether the benefit persists when
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is combined with the existing framework
components (data selection and DepthMix). The main cross-task loss
experiments are conducted on the Cityscapes dataset at the 372-label
(1/8) regime, while the reproduction tables also include the additional
label regimes reported in the original paper. Results are reported as
mean mIoU~(\%) $\pm$ standard deviation across three independent seeds
(7, 25, 42). Reproduction entries are presented as
ours~/~{\scriptsize (paper)}, followed by the absolute discrepancy
$\Delta = \text{ours} - \text{paper}$.

% ============================================================
\section{Reproduction Protocol}
\label{sec:repro_protocol}

\noindent
To avoid redundancy, this evaluation follows the design and implementation
described in Chapter~\ref{chap:execution}. Stage~(a) and transfer
experiments use a ResNet-101 encoder with an ASPP-based decoder;
Stage~(b) multi-task and cross-task loss experiments additionally employ
the PAD decoder (Section~\ref{sec:pad-decoder}). We used the
author-provided fixed subset indices for the 100, 372, and 744 label
regimes. All models were trained for 40,000 iterations using SGD with
polynomial learning-rate decay, and results are reported as mean
mIoU~(\%) $\pm$ standard deviation over three seeds. For Stage~(b)
transfer-related experiments (Table~5), we use the author-released SDE
checkpoints (fd0/fd2) to maintain parity with the official pipeline.

Baseline values are reported per table under the corresponding experiment
configuration. When baseline values differ across tables, this reflects
differences in the surrounding training pipeline rather than implying
direct numerical comparability across tables.

All experiments in this chapter were conducted under the single-GPU
constraint described in Section~\ref{sec:setup}: batch size~2 without
Synchronised Batch Normalisation~(SyncBN). As discussed in
Chapter~\ref{chap:execution}, this reduces the number of samples
available to each batch normalisation step, which is a known source of
instability for semi-supervised methods. This provides the unified
background context for the reproduction gaps observed across subsequent
tables; the following sections quantify these gaps per configuration
without repeating this explanation.

selection improves over random sampling is consistently reproduced.

\noindent
\textbf{Observation and Analysis.}
The performance hierarchy (DepthMix $>$ ClassMix $>$ Baseline) is
reproduced across both label regimes. The result is consistent with the
interpretation that incorporating geometric scene structure into the
mixing mask may reduce the boundary artefacts typical of ClassMix, where
semantically inconsistent regions can be introduced at the cut boundary.
The largest deviation from the paper is observed for pseudo-labelling
($\Delta = -2.34$), which is considerably larger than all other gaps in
this table. One plausible explanation is that pseudo-label self-training
relies on per-batch confidence statistics to filter low-quality
pseudo-labels at each update step; with a smaller batch, these statistics
are estimated from fewer samples and may therefore be less stable,
allowing more erroneous pseudo-labels to enter training and making
self-training more susceptible to confirmation bias. By contrast, the
mixing masks of ClassMix and DepthMix are derived directly from class
segments or geometric depth estimates, without depending on confidence
statistics, and are thus likely to be more robust to batch size variation.
This observation provides a practical motivation for considering
augmentation-based and multi-task alternatives, which appear more stable
under the same constraint.

\textbf{Observation and Analysis.}
Our results are broadly consistent with the core scientific claims of the
original paper: SDE pretraining appears to provide a stronger
initialisation, and $\mathcal{L}_F$ may help prevent catastrophic
forgetting of the geometric representations acquired during self-supervised
depth estimation. The near-zero gap for Transfer ($\mathcal{L}_F=\checkmark$)
at 372 labels ($\Delta = +0.03$) is encouraging, suggesting that the
benefit of SDE-derived features transfers robustly across training
environments even under single-GPU constraints.

These results also point to a structural limitation of the MTL setting.
Although the depth and segmentation decoders share the same encoder and
are trained jointly, their intermediate feature representations are never
explicitly required to be mutually consistent. The depth decoder learns
geometric structure that is potentially informative for segmentation
boundaries and object extents, yet this information can only propagate
implicitly through the shared encoder gradients. Consequently, the extent
to which depth-derived geometric knowledge benefits the segmentation
branch depends on the gradient dynamics of the shared encoder, which
remain implicit and are difficult to control directly. This structural
gap provides a clear motivation for the design of
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$, which introduces a learnable projection head to
enforce explicit alignment between the two decoder branches, providing a
more direct and controllable channel for geometric knowledge to
regularise the segmentation task under label-efficient conditions.

The full ablation indicates that S+DX+MTL achieves the highest performance
at the 372-label regime ($67.66$\,\% mIoU), consistent with the paper's
findings. Our reproduced results broadly recover the qualitative trends
reported in the paper, with most configurations falling within
$\pm$1\,mIoU, and several single-GPU runs marginally exceeding the
reported values---notably S\,+\,MTL (without DX, $\Delta = +1.00$\,pp)
and S\,+\,DX (without MTL, $\Delta = +0.51$\,pp)---which may reflect
favourable random-seed interactions under the fixed subset indices.

A notable discrepancy arises in the DX+MTL configuration at 372 labels,
where our result ($65.11$) falls $1.55$\,mIoU below the paper ($66.66$),
despite exceeding the DX-only result ($64.57$). One plausible explanation
is that the joint optimisation of DepthMix augmentation and the auxiliary
depth objective is more sensitive to batch size than either component in
isolation. Specifically, DepthMix introduces mixed-geometry boundary
regions whose depth supervision may be noisier than in unmixed samples.
Under a small batch size, the auxiliary depth objective may receive noisier
gradient estimates from these boundary regions, which could interfere with
the segmentation branch via the shared encoder. In the multi-GPU setup of
the original paper, larger effective batch sizes may smooth out this noise,
whereas the single-GPU setup may amplify it.

This pattern reinforces the structural gap identified in Table~5: under
small-batch conditions, the implicit path through the shared encoder may be
particularly susceptible to gradient noise introduced by mixed-geometry
samples. An explicit feature-level alignment loss operating directly on
decoder representations---after the augmentation-induced noise has been
absorbed by the task-specific heads---may therefore provide a more stable
channel for cross-task knowledge transfer in this setting.

\section{Cross-Task Consistency Loss: Calibration and Ablation}
\label{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}

\noindent
Building on the reproduced PAD-MTL baseline, this section evaluates the
proposed Cross-Task Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), which
aligns intermediate features from the segmentation and depth decoder
branches via a learnable $1{\times}1$ convolutional projection head.
We report results from two experimental phases: a single-seed calibration
sweep (Phase~I) over loss type, projection layout, projection direction,
and $\lambda_\text{ct}$; and a three-seed validation (Phase~II) for the
most promising configurations. All $\Delta$ values are reported as
absolute mIoU differences in percentage points~(pp).

% ============================================================
\subsection{Phase I: Single-seed Calibration (Seed~7)}
\label{subsec:ct-phase1-seed7}

\noindent
The Phase~I sweep for $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ covers three factors:
loss type (cosine similarity vs.\ normalised MSE), projection layout
(single vs.\ dual $1{\times}1$ convolutional heads), and
$\lambda_\text{ct}\in\{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$. For the single
projection layout, two projection directions are additionally evaluated:
single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$, in which the depth feature is projected into
the segmentation space, and single~$(s{\rightarrow}d)$, in which the
segmentation feature is projected into the depth space. This yields
$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 4 = 32$ configurations in total, but since
the dual projection layout has no direction variant, the effective count
is $2 \times 4 + 2 \times 4 + 1 \times 2 \times 4 = 24$ distinct
configurations.

Phase~I fixes random seed~7 to screen all configurations; the goal is
not to identify a globally optimal configuration, but to eliminate
underperforming regions of the search space and reduce the candidate
set to a tractable size for multi-seed validation in Phase~II.
Seed~7 was selected because it corresponds to the median-performing seed
in the PAD-MTL baseline ($59.84$\,\%, compared to $60.92$\,\% and
$61.50$\,\% for seeds~25 and~42), providing a conservative reference.
All Phase~I $\Delta$ values use $\text{baseline}_{\text{S7}} = 59.84$\,\%.

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-I findings.}
All 24 configurations improve over the seed-7 baseline, confirming that
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ provides a positive signal across the tested
projection layouts, directions, loss types, and $\lambda_\text{ct}$ values.

For single projection, the $d{\rightarrow}s$ direction achieves a higher
Phase~I peak than $s{\rightarrow}d$: $63.77$\,\% vs.\ $63.08$\,\%
respectively. For $d{\rightarrow}s$, cosine loss with
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ reaches the per-direction maximum. In contrast,
for $s{\rightarrow}d$, the cosine peak occurs at $\lambda_\text{ct}=0.25$
($62.70$\,\%), and higher $\lambda_\text{ct}$ values reduce performance
(down to $61.26$--$61.30$\,\% at $\lambda=0.75/1.00$). This indicates
that the $s{\rightarrow}d$ direction is more sensitive to
$\lambda_\text{ct}$ in the single-seed screen.

For dual projection, the peak is $63.66$\,\% (cosine, $\lambda=0.50$),
which is competitive with but below the single $d{\rightarrow}s$ peak.
The dual MSE results are more stable across $\lambda_\text{ct}$ values
than dual cosine. Although the dual-projection peaks are notable,
Phase~I is a single-seed screen; whether these peaks generalise is
evaluated in Phase~II.

Based on these results, Phase~II carries forward selected high-performing
regions from Phase~I: single $d{\rightarrow}s$ cosine and MSE around the
best-performing loss scales; single $s{\rightarrow}d$ cosine and MSE
around their strongest Phase~I settings; and dual cosine at
$\lambda \in \{0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$. This enables a direct comparison of
projection direction, projection layout, and loss formulation under
multi-seed conditions.

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-II findings.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textit{$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is robustly beneficial across the
  tested configurations.}
  All thirteen validated configurations improve over the baseline mean
  ($60.75$\,\%), with gains ranging from $+1.55$ to $+2.81$\,pp.
  This is consistent with $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ providing a reliable
  positive signal across the tested architectural choices and
  hyperparameter settings.

\item \textit{The highest raw mean does not correspond to the most stable
  configuration.}
  Single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ MSE at $\lambda=1.00$ achieves the highest
  mean of $63.56$\,\%, outperforming the selected cosine variant by
  $0.31$\,pp. However, its standard deviation is substantially larger
  ($0.95$\,\% vs.\ $0.17$\,\%), and the high mean is strongly influenced
  by seed~42 ($64.91$\,\%). By contrast, single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$
  cosine at $\lambda=1.00$ achieves a slightly lower mean
  ($63.25$\,\%) but with tightly clustered seed values
  ($63.01$ / $63.37$ / $63.37$). For this reason, the cosine variant is
  treated as the more reliable configuration.

\item \textit{Projection direction affects stability and loss-scale
  sensitivity.}
  The best $s{\rightarrow}d$ configuration in Phase~II is cosine with
  $\lambda=0.75$, achieving $63.12$\,\% with std $0.63$\,\%.
  This is competitive with but slightly below the selected
  $d{\rightarrow}s$ cosine configuration. In contrast,
  $s{\rightarrow}d$ cosine at $\lambda=1.00$ drops to $62.30$\,\%,
  suggesting that the reverse direction is more sensitive to the loss
  scale. This supports the interpretation that projecting depth features
  into the segmentation space provides a more stable optimisation target
  in the PAD-MTL baseline setting.

\item \textit{MSE loss produces competitive means but higher variance
  than cosine.}
  MSE variants achieve strong raw performance, particularly
  single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ MSE at $\lambda=1.00$ ($63.56$\,\%) and
  single~$(s{\rightarrow}d)$ MSE at $\lambda=1.00$ ($62.88$\,\%).
  However, their standard deviations are considerably higher than the
  best cosine variants. This suggests that MSE is more sensitive to
  random initialisation and training dynamics, whereas cosine similarity
  provides a more stable angular alignment objective across seeds.

\item \textit{Single $(d{\rightarrow}s)$ cosine, $\lambda=1.00$ is
  selected as the primary configuration.}
  This configuration achieves mean $63.25$\,\% ($\Delta=+2.50$\,pp)
  with the lowest standard deviation of any tested variant ($0.17$\,\%),
  with seed values tightly clustered at $63.01$ / $63.37$ / $63.37$.
  This stability indicates the improvement is consistent and
  reproducible rather than seed-dependent, and it is therefore
  selected as the primary design choice for downstream experiments.

\item \textit{Dual-projection results fall below the best stable
  single-projection result.}
  The best dual variant (dual cosine, $\lambda=0.75$) achieves mean
  $62.95$\,\% ($+2.20$\,pp), approximately $0.30$\,pp below the
  selected single $(d{\rightarrow}s)$ configuration, while doubling
  the additional parameters from $16{,}512$ to $33{,}024$.
  The asymmetric structure of single projection---where one branch
  remains in its original feature space while the other is projected---may
  provide a more constrained alignment target than dual projection, where
  both feature spaces are allowed to move simultaneously.

\item \textit{Phase~I single-seed peaks do not always generalise.}
  The Phase~I peak for dual cosine ($\lambda=0.50$: $63.66$\,\%)
  drops to a three-seed mean of $62.87$\,\%, a gap of $0.79$\,pp.
  Similarly, the best Phase~I $s{\rightarrow}d$ setting
  (MSE, $\lambda=1.00$: $63.08$\,\%) reaches a lower three-seed mean
  of $62.88$\,\%. This underscores the necessity of multi-seed
  validation before drawing conclusions on optimal $\lambda_\text{ct}$,
  and supports the two-phase design of the evaluation protocol.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary and Conclusions}
\label{subsec:ct-summary}

\noindent
The two-phase evaluation supports the following conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textit{Effectiveness.} Across all validated configurations,
  $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ improves over the PAD-MTL baseline,
  indicating that explicit cross-task feature alignment is beneficial
  for label-efficient segmentation within the present setup. The gains
  are robust across the tested projection layouts, projection directions,
  loss types, and $\lambda_\text{ct}$ values.

\item \textit{Single projection with depth-to-seg alignment is the most
  reliable design on the PAD-MTL baseline.}
  The highest raw mean is obtained by single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ MSE
  with $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ ($63.56$\,\%), but its standard
  deviation is substantially larger than that of the cosine variant
  ($0.95$\,\% vs.\ $0.17$\,\%). The strongest stable configuration is
  therefore single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ cosine with
  $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$, which achieves mean $63.25$\,\% with the
  lowest observed variance. The best $s{\rightarrow}d$ variant, cosine
  with $\lambda_\text{ct}=0.75$, remains competitive ($63.12$\,\%) but
  does not exceed the selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ cosine configuration in
  either mean performance or stability.

\item \textit{Cosine loss shows more reliable behaviour than MSE.}
  MSE variants achieve competitive or even higher raw means, but their
  standard deviations are consistently higher than the most stable cosine
  configuration. Cosine similarity, being scale-invariant, provides a
  more stable alignment objective across random seeds, whereas MSE is
  more sensitive to seed-dependent feature magnitude and optimisation
  dynamics.

\item \textit{Stable $\lambda_\text{ct}$ range.}
  For single $(d{\rightarrow}s)$ cosine, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$
  offers the best balance of performance and stability across three
  seeds. The adjacent value of $0.75$ still improves over the baseline
  with low variance, indicating that the method is not highly sensitive
  to the exact choice of $\lambda_\text{ct}$ within this range. However,
  as shown in Table~\ref{tab:ct-phase2-3seeds}, the $s{\rightarrow}d$
  direction shows stronger sensitivity to the loss scale, with cosine
  performance dropping notably at $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ (from
  $63.12$\,\% at $\lambda=0.75$ to $62.30$\,\% at $\lambda=1.00$).

\end{enumerate}

\section{Cross-Task Consistency Loss: Composability with Framework Components}
\label{sec:ct-component-ablation}

\noindent
The calibration study in Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}
identified single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ projection with cosine similarity
and $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ as the most reliable configuration on top
of the PAD-MTL baseline. This section evaluates whether the benefit of
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ persists when it is combined with the remaining
framework components, namely data selection~(S) and DepthMix~(DX). Since
Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation} selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ as
the primary and most stable projection direction, the main composability
analysis reports only the selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ single-projection
track.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Objective.}
We ask whether $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ continues to improve segmentation
performance once stronger baselines are introduced, or whether its
benefit is subsumed by other components. This is a composability
question: even if $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ improves over pure PAD-MTL,
its contribution could be redundant with the geometric priors introduced
by DepthMix or with the higher-quality supervision introduced by data
selection. The ablation therefore covers three component combinations on
top of PAD-MTL: MTL\,+\,DX, MTL\,+\,S, and MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S. For each
combination, the main reported ablation compares the matching baseline
against the selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ single-projection variants using
both loss types (MSE and cosine). This reporting choice follows the
Phase~II calibration, where $d{\rightarrow}s$ cosine was identified as
the most reliable direction on the PAD-MTL baseline. All
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ variants use single projection,
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$, and bidirectional gradient flow.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Design of the mean-level comparison.}
The baseline rows in each group of
Table~\ref{tab:ct-component-ablation} are trained under the same
pipeline and in the same submission batch as the corresponding
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ variants. Each $\Delta$ is reported as the
difference between the three-seed mean of the $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$
variant and the three-seed mean of the matching
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$-free baseline. This controls for the framework
configuration, although the comparison should be interpreted as a
mean-level component ablation rather than a formal statistical test.

\textbf{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textit{$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ shows positive mean-level gains
  across all three component combinations in the selected
  $d{\rightarrow}s$ track.}
  All six reported $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ variants improve over their
  matching baselines in terms of three-seed mean mIoU, with gains ranging
  from $+0.10$ to $+0.97$\,pp. However, the smallest gain is within the
  observed seed variation and should be interpreted cautiously. Overall,
  the mean-level trend suggests that the proposed loss can remain useful
  under stronger baselines, rather than being fully subsumed by data
  selection or DepthMix.

\item \textit{$d{\rightarrow}s$ is retained as the primary direction for
  controlled composability analysis.}
  The Phase~II calibration identified single $(d{\rightarrow}s)$ cosine
  as the most reliable direction on the PAD-MTL baseline. In the
  component-level ablation, the same direction remains positive in mean
  across all three framework combinations: $+0.97$\,pp on MTL\,+\,DX with
  cosine, $+0.60$\,pp on MTL\,+\,S with MSE, and $+0.53$\,pp on
  MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S with MSE. The main ablation table therefore focuses
  on the calibration-selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ direction to keep the
  comparison controlled and interpretable.

\item \textit{The largest benefit arises on MTL\,+\,DX, which is the
  configuration with the largest reproduction gap.}
  The reproduced MTL\,+\,DX baseline lies $1.55$\,pp below the paper
  (Table~\ref{tab:table7_repro}), the most pronounced discrepancy in
  the Table~7 reproduction. Adding $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ recovers a
  substantial fraction of this gap: the best variant
  ($d{\rightarrow}s$ cosine) closes it by $+0.97$\,pp. This is
  consistent with the interpretation that DepthMix may produce
  mixed-geometry boundary regions whose depth supervision is noisier
  under small batch sizes, and an explicit alignment loss may provide a
  more stable channel for geometric knowledge transfer when the
  implicit path through the shared encoder is disrupted.

\item \textit{Within the selected $d{\rightarrow}s$ track, MSE--cosine
  ordering depends on whether DepthMix is present.}
  On MTL\,+\,S, MSE achieves higher gains than cosine for the
  $d{\rightarrow}s$ direction ($+0.60$ vs.\ $+0.10$\,pp). When
  DepthMix is introduced, cosine becomes stronger on MTL\,+\,DX
  ($+0.97$\,pp vs.\ $+0.66$\,pp), while on MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S the MSE
  variant reaches the higher mean within $d{\rightarrow}s$
  ($68.19$\,\% vs.\ $67.93$\,\%). This indicates that the preferred loss
  type is component-dependent even after fixing projection direction.

\item \textit{Within the selected direction, the strongest configuration is
  $d{\rightarrow}s$ MSE on MTL\,+\,DX\,+\,S.}
  This configuration attains $68.19$\,\%, exceeding the reproduced
  full-framework baseline ($67.66$\,\%) by $+0.53$\,pp. Compared with the
  paper's reported S\,+\,DX\,+\,MTL value ($68.01$\,\%), the difference is
  $+0.18$\,pp, which is small relative to observed seed variation.
  Therefore, the appropriate interpretation is that the single-GPU result
  is comparable to the multi-GPU reference, rather than a definitive
  outperformance claim.

\end{itemize}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent
\textbf{Summary.}
This ablation suggests that the benefit of $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$
observed in Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation} is not specific to
the pure PAD-MTL setting. All three component combinations show positive
mean-level gains from the proposed loss within the selected
$d{\rightarrow}s$ track; the gain is largest where the reproduction is
weakest (MTL\,+\,DX); and the best full-framework configuration within
this selected track becomes comparable to the original paper's reported
value under the constrained single-GPU setting. Accordingly, the main
composability analysis retains only the calibration-selected
$d{\rightarrow}s$ single-projection track for final comparison.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:chapter4_summary}

\noindent
This chapter has evaluated the project's experimental work from two
perspectives: the quality of the reproduction of the original framework,
and the effectiveness of the proposed $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ extension.

With respect to reproduction, the main qualitative performance trends from
the four result tables (Tables~1, 3, 5, and~7) are largely preserved under
the single-GPU constraint. The reproduced results broadly recover the
findings of \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde}, with most configurations falling
within $\pm$1\,mIoU of the paper and several single-GPU runs marginally
exceeding the reported values. Where under-reproduction occurs, the
reduced batch size and absence of SyncBN provide plausible contributing
factors, since both are known to affect the stability of semi-supervised
training.

With respect to the extension, $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ produces positive
mean improvements across all validated configurations. The highest raw
Phase~II mean is obtained by single~$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ MSE,
$\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$, but its higher variance makes it less reliable
for model selection. The calibrated primary design---single
$(d{\rightarrow}s)$ cosine, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$---achieves a
consistent, low-variance gain of $+2.50$\,pp over the PAD-MTL baseline
across three independent seeds. Based on this calibration, the final
composability analysis focuses on the selected $d{\rightarrow}s$
single-projection track. When combined with data selection and DepthMix,
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ shows positive mean-level gains within this
selected track, supporting the conclusion that the improvement is not
limited to the isolated PAD-MTL baseline and remains observable under the
constrained single-GPU setting used in this project.
