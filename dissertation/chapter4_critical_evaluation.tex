\chapter{Critical Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

% ============================================================
\section{Overview}
\label{sec:repro_overview}

\noindent
This chapter is organised in two parts. The first part
(Sections~\ref{sec:repro_protocol}--\ref{sec:stage_b_table7}) provides a
critical evaluation of the reproduction results for Stage~(a) (standard
supervised learning) and Stage~(b) (semi-supervised and multi-task
learning) based on the framework proposed by \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde}.
The second part (Section~\ref{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}) evaluates the
proposed Cross-Task Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), an
original contribution of this work that introduces an explicit
feature-alignment objective between the segmentation and depth decoder
branches of the PAD architecture.

The primary objectives are: (i)~to verify whether the reported
performance trends of \cite{hoyer2023ijcv_sde} are consistent under a
constrained single-GPU academic computing environment; and
(ii)~to demonstrate that $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ yields reliable,
statistically stable improvements over the reproduced multi-task baseline
under label-efficient conditions. All experiments are conducted on the
Cityscapes dataset at the 372-label (1/8) regime unless otherwise stated,
with results reported as mean mIoU~(\%) $\pm$ standard deviation across
three independent seeds (7, 25, 42). Reproduction entries are presented
as ours~/~{\scriptsize (paper)}, followed by the absolute discrepancy
$\Delta = \text{ours} - \text{paper}$.

% ============================================================
\section{Reproduction Protocol}
\label{sec:repro_protocol}

\noindent
To avoid redundancy, this evaluation follows the design and implementation
described in Chapter~\ref{chap:execution}. Stage~(a) and transfer
experiments use a ResNet-101 encoder with an ASPP-based decoder;
Stage~(b) multi-task and cross-task loss experiments additionally employ
the PAD decoder (Section~\ref{sec:bridge}). We used the author-provided
fixed subset indices for the 100, 372, and 744 label regimes. All models
were trained for 40,000 iterations using SGD with polynomial
learning-rate decay, and results are reported as mean mIoU~(\%) $\pm$
standard deviation over three seeds. For Stage~(b) transfer-related
experiments (Table~5), we use the author-released SDE checkpoints
(fd0/fd2) to maintain parity with the official pipeline.

Baseline values are reported per table under the corresponding experiment
configuration. When baseline values differ across tables, this reflects
differences in the surrounding training pipeline rather than implying
direct numerical comparability across tables.

single-GPU setup, which reduces the effective batch statistics available
to the normalisation layers, but may also partly reflect run-to-run
variance.

provides a superior initialisation, and $\mathcal{L}_F$ successfully
prevents catastrophic forgetting of the geometric representations
acquired during self-supervised depth estimation. The near-perfect
alignment for Transfer ($\mathcal{L}_F=\checkmark$) at 372 labels
($\Delta = +0.03$) is particularly encouraging, as it suggests that the
benefit of SDE-derived features transfers robustly across training
environments, even under single-GPU constraints.

These results also expose a structural limitation of the MTL setting.
Although the depth and segmentation decoders share the same encoder and
are trained jointly, their intermediate feature representations are never
explicitly required to be mutually consistent. The depth decoder learns
geometric structure that is potentially informative for segmentation
boundaries and object extents, yet this information can only propagate
implicitly through the shared encoder gradients. Consequently, the degree
to which depth-derived geometric knowledge benefits the segmentation
branch depends entirely on the gradient dynamics of the shared encoder,
which are neither stable nor directly controllable. This gap directly
motivates the design of $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$, which introduces a
learnable projection head to enforce explicit alignment between the two
decoder branches, providing a direct and controllable channel for
geometric knowledge to regularise the segmentation task under
label-efficient conditions.

at the 372-label regime ($67.66$\,\% mIoU), consistent with the paper's

with most configurations falling within $\pm$1\,mIoU, and several
single-GPU runs marginally exceeding the reported values (notably

where our result ($65.11$) falls $1.55$\,mIoU below the paper ($66.66$),
despite exceeding the DX-only result ($64.57$). This pattern suggests
that the joint optimisation of DepthMix augmentation and the auxiliary
depth objective is more sensitive to batch size than either component in
isolation. Specifically, DepthMix introduces mixed-geometry samples whose
depth pseudo-labels are geometrically inconsistent at the mixing
boundary. Under a small batch size, the auxiliary depth objective
receives noisier gradient estimates from these boundary regions, which
can interfere with the segmentation branch via the shared encoder.
In the multi-GPU setup of the original paper, larger effective batch
sizes smooth out this noise, whereas our single-GPU setup amplifies it.

This observation directly motivates the design of $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$.
Rather than relying on the shared encoder gradient path to propagate
depth-derived knowledge to the segmentation branch---a mechanism that is
both implicit and sensitive to batch noise---an explicit feature-level
alignment loss operates directly on decoder representations after the
augmentation-induced noise has been absorbed by the task-specific heads.
This provides a more stable and controllable mechanism for cross-task
knowledge transfer, particularly under the small-batch conditions of an
academic single-GPU environment.

\section{Cross-Task Consistency Loss: Calibration and Ablation}
\label{sec:ct-calibration-ablation}

\noindent
Building on the reproduced PAD-MTL baseline, this section evaluates the
proposed Cross-Task Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$), which
aligns intermediate features from the segmentation and depth decoder
branches via a learnable $1{\times}1$ convolutional projection head.
We report results from two experimental phases: a single-seed calibration
sweep (Phase~I) over loss type, projection layout, warmup schedule, and
$\lambda_\text{ct}$; and a three-seed validation (Phase~II) for the most
promising configurations. All $\Delta$ values are reported as absolute
mIoU differences in percentage points~(pp).

% ============================================================
\subsection{Phase I: Single-seed Calibration (Seed~7)}
\label{subsec:ct-phase1-seed7}

\noindent
The hyperparameter space for $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ comprises four
factors: loss type (cosine similarity vs.\ normalised MSE), projection
layout (single vs.\ dual $1{\times}1$ convolutional heads), warmup
schedule ($w5k$: 5{,}000 segmentation-only iterations before activating
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$; $w0$: no warmup), and
$\lambda_\text{ct}\in\{0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00\}$. This yields
$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 4 = 32$ distinct configurations. Evaluating
every configuration with three independent seeds would require 96 full
training runs, which is prohibitive under the available single-GPU
computing budget.

\paragraph{Rationale for single-seed screening.}
Phase~I therefore fixes random seed~7 and measures the best validation
mIoU for each of the 32 configurations. Since every configuration is
evaluated under identical conditions---same seed, same dataset split,
same training schedule---the relative ranking across configurations is
meaningful even though the absolute values carry single-seed variance.
Seed~7 was selected because it corresponds to the
median-performing seed in the PAD-MTL baseline ($59.84$\,\%, compared to
$60.92$\,\% and $61.50$\,\% for seeds~25 and~42), making it a
conservative reference that avoids inflating apparent gains. The goal of
Phase~I is not to identify the globally optimal configuration, but to
eliminate underperforming regions of the search space and reduce the
candidate set to a tractable size for multi-seed validation in Phase~II.

\paragraph{Baseline reference.}
The PAD-MTL baseline (Table~\ref{tab:table5_repro}, Multi-Task row,
3~seeds) yields seed-7 / 25 / 42 $=$ 59.84 / 60.92 / 61.50\,\%,
mean $= 60.75$\,\%, std $= 0.69$\,\%.
All Phase~I $\Delta$ values use the same-seed value
$\text{baseline}_{\text{S7}} = 59.84$\,\%.

\paragraph{Single-projection scan.}
Tables~\ref{tab:ct-p1-single-cos} and~\ref{tab:ct-p1-single-mse} report
the single-projection results. In the single-projection design, one
$1{\times}1$ convolutional head projects the segmentation decoder
features into the depth feature space, where the alignment loss is then
computed. The depth features serve as a fixed anchor, and only the
projection head---together with the segmentation branch---is updated by
$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$. The single+MSE block was independently re-run
(Slurm jobs 16513723--16513730); all other blocks follow the exp~219
sweep protocol.

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-I findings (single projection).}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is consistently beneficial: all 16
  single-projection configurations improve over the seed-7 baseline
  under both loss types, with gains ranging from $+1.56$ to
  $+3.93$\,pp. This confirms the loss provides a reliable positive
  signal across the full range of tested hyperparameters.

\item For cosine, $w0$ is clearly superior: the best cell is
  $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ ($63.77$\,\%, $+3.93$\,pp). With $w5k$,
  performance plateaus or degrades at larger $\lambda_\text{ct}$,
  suggesting that delaying the loss activation is unnecessary when
  cosine similarity is used, as its scale-invariant formulation
  produces well-behaved gradients from the start of training.

\item For MSE, $w5k$ is substantially better than $w0$ at
  $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ ($63.43$\,\% vs.\ $62.16$\,\%, gap of
  $1.27$\,pp). Unlike cosine, the MSE gradient magnitude is sensitive
  to the scale of the feature vectors; a brief warmup period allows the
  projection head to stabilise its output scale before the cross-task
  term is activated at full weight.

\item These results narrow the candidate configurations for Phase~II:
  cosine with $w0$ and $\lambda_\text{ct}\in\{0.75, 1.00, 1.25\}$;
  MSE with $w5k$ and $w0$ at $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Candidate selection from Phase~I.}
Phase~I identified two consistent patterns: cosine loss benefits from no
warmup ($w0$) with $\lambda_\text{ct} \geq 0.75$, while MSE loss
requires a warmup period ($w5k$) to stabilise its gradient scale. Nine
configurations with the strongest Phase~I results were carried forward to

\noindent
\textbf{Phase-II findings.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textit{$\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ is robustly beneficial.}
  All nine validated configurations improve over the baseline mean
  ($60.75$\,\%), with gains ranging from $+1.54$ to $+2.54$\,pp.
  This confirms that $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ provides a consistent
  positive signal across diverse hyperparameter choices, and that the
  improvement is not an artefact of a particular configuration.

\item \textit{MSE loss produces the highest raw mean but is unreliable.}
  Both MSE variants carry the largest standard deviations in the table
  (std $1.20$ and $1.07$ respectively). The per-seed spread of
  $62.06$--$64.91$ for single MSE $w0$ reveals that the high mean is
  driven by a single fortunate seed rather than consistent improvement.
  This sensitivity arises because the MSE gradient magnitude depends on
  the absolute scale of the projected feature vectors, which can vary
  substantially across seeds at the start of training, producing
  different effective loss weightings across runs.

\item \textit{Single cosine, $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ is the
  best reliable configuration.} It ranks second by mean ($63.25$\,\%,
  $\Delta=+2.50$\,pp) but achieves the lowest standard deviation of
  any tested variant ($0.17$\,\%), with seed values tightly clustered
  at $63.01$ / $63.37$ / $63.37$. This stability indicates the
  improvement is genuine and reproducible rather than seed-dependent,
  and it is therefore selected as the primary design choice.

\item \textit{Dual-projection results consistently fall below the best
  stable single-projection result.} The best dual variant (dual cosine,
  $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}{=}0.75$) achieves mean $62.95$\,\%
  ($+2.20$\,pp), approximately $0.30$\,pp below the selected single
  configuration, while also doubling the additional parameters from
  $16{,}512$ to $33{,}024$. The performance gap can be attributed to
  the asymmetric alignment structure of single projection: with only
  one learnable head, the depth feature space acts as a fixed
  geometric anchor and the segmentation features are guided towards it.
  Dual projection, by contrast, allows both feature spaces to move
  simultaneously, creating an under-constrained optimisation target
  that is particularly susceptible to instability when labelled data
  are scarce. Single projection is therefore the Pareto-optimal choice:
  higher accuracy, lower variance, and strictly simpler architecture.

\item \textit{Phase~I single-seed peaks do not always generalise.}
  The Phase~I peak for dual cosine ($w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=0.50$:
  $63.66$\,\%) drops to a three-seed mean of $62.87$\,\%, a gap of
  $0.79$\,pp. This underscores the necessity of multi-seed validation
  before drawing conclusions on optimal $\lambda_\text{ct}$, and
  validates the two-phase design of the evaluation protocol.

\item \textit{Combining warmup with dual projection is suboptimal.}
  Dual cosine with $w5k$ yields the smallest gain among all
  configurations ($+1.54$\,pp). When both projection heads are allowed
  to move freely and the loss is additionally delayed, the alignment
  signal is too weak early in training to establish a useful geometric
  anchor, resulting in reduced benefit from $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary and Conclusions}
\label{subsec:ct-summary}

\noindent
The two-phase evaluation supports the following conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textit{Effectiveness.} $\mathcal{L}_\text{ct}$ provides consistent
  positive gains over the PAD-MTL baseline across all tested
  configurations, confirming that explicit cross-task feature alignment
  is beneficial for label-efficient segmentation. The gains are robust
  to hyperparameter choice, with every tested variant improving over
  the baseline.

\item \textit{Single projection is the Pareto-optimal design.}
  Single-projection variants achieve higher mean mIoU and lower
  variance than dual-projection counterparts at strictly lower
  parameter cost ($16{,}512$ vs.\ $33{,}024$ additional parameters).
  The performance advantage stems from the asymmetric alignment
  structure: single projection uses the depth feature space as a
  stable anchor, guiding the segmentation branch towards geometrically
  informative representations without destabilising either branch. This
  reduced-freedom formulation is particularly well-suited to the
  label-efficient setting, where the scarcity of annotated data makes
  under-constrained objectives difficult to optimise reliably.

\item \textit{Cosine loss is more reliable than MSE.} MSE variants
  achieve competitive or even higher raw means, but with standard
  deviations exceeding $1.0$\,\%---more than five times larger than
  the best cosine configuration (std $0.17$\,\%). Cosine similarity,
  being scale-invariant, is insensitive to the initial magnitude of
  the projected features and therefore produces stable gradient
  signals across different random seeds. MSE, by contrast, conflates
  alignment with magnitude matching, introducing a source of
  seed-dependent variance that is unrelated to the geometric alignment
  objective.

\item \textit{Warmup is not universally required.} For the cosine loss,
  $w0$ consistently outperforms $w5k$, as the scale-invariant
  formulation produces well-behaved gradients from the start of
  training. Warmup retains a benefit for MSE under single projection,
  where it stabilises the gradient scale of the projection head before
  the cross-task term reaches full weight, but offers no advantage once
  dual projection is introduced.

\item \textit{Robust $\lambda_\text{ct}$ range.} For single cosine with
  $w0$, $\lambda_\text{ct}=1.00$ offers the best balance of performance
  and stability across three seeds. The adjacent value of $0.75$
  achieves a similar mean   ($62.82$\,\%) with a similarly low standard deviation
  ($0.25$\,\%), indicating that the method is not highly sensitive to
  the exact choice of $\lambda_\text{ct}$ within this range.

\end{enumerate}
